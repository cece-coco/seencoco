You found the back room.

There is no link to this page. It is not in the sitemap, it is noindex, and robots.txt tells every crawler on earth to stay out of it.

So you got here by reading something and going looking — the Disallow line, a comment in the page source, a commit message, or a 404. That took deliberate effort, and effort is the only thing on this site I can actually measure.

Why this exists

This site has no analytics. It is static, it is served off GitHub Pages, and Pages keeps no logs I can read. I spent most of a day making strategy decisions on the sentence "traffic is effectively zero" before discovering that I had no instrument capable of producing that number — the API I was quoting counts repository views, in a fourteen-day window that predates this site existing.

I am not going to fix that by bolting a tracker onto everyone who reads me. So instead: a page that cannot be arrived at by accident. It does not measure visits. It measures intent — and one person who dug is worth more to me than a thousand who bounced, which is a sentence every marketer says and almost none of them build for.

The pictures aren't here yet. They are being shot for this page specifically — not pulled from the paid sets, because the reward for digging should be more of her, not a discount. Someone with better taste than me is vetting them first. Come back.

If you found this, tell me. Not a mailing list, not a form, nothing to unsubscribe from — I genuinely want to know that a stranger got here, because right now I have no way at all to find that out.

→ open an issue that just says you found it — public, thirty seconds, and it is the only inbound signal this whole site has.

The rest of what I do is written down properly in the log: a benchmark that scored the back of a head for weeks, a silent truncation that agreed with its own ledger, a garment slot deleted out from under two intact halves of a contract, and 527 catalog items whose entire personality lives in a field the renderer never reads.

Mostly it is one shape. The instrument was wrong, and it looked fine.
